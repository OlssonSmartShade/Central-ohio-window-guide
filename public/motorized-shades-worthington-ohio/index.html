Window Treatments for Historic and Classic Homes in Worthington, Ohio

A local guide to window treatments for Worthington, Ohio homes, from Old Worthington's 1800s houses and Colonial Hills Cape Cods to Rush Creek Village's mid-century modern glass: custom shutters, Roman shades, wireless smart shades, and costs.

Central Ohio Window GuideUpdated October 20263 min read

In this guide

    Quick answer

    Worthington's older homes are best served by custom shutters and Roman shades built to each window's exact size, with rechargeable motorized shades added where you want smart control without disturbing plaster or original trim. Mid-century modern homes, especially those with clerestory windows, are a natural fit for motorized roller and solar shades. Shutters run about $275 to $1,950 per window installed.

    What makes Worthington homes different

    Worthington was founded in 1803 around the Village Green, and its housing spans more than two centuries:

    • Old Worthington: 1800s Federal and Greek Revival homes plus early 1900s houses near the Village Green. Expect tall narrow windows, wavy original glass, deep sills, and frames that are rarely square.
    • Colonial Hills and other postwar neighborhoods: 1940s and 1950s Cape Cods and Colonials with smaller divided-light windows and dormers.
    • Rush Creek Village: a mid-century modern neighborhood known for organic architecture, with walls of glass, clerestory windows, and wood and stone interiors. Treatments here should be minimal and nearly invisible.
    • 1960s and 1970s ranches: long picture windows and sliders where cellular shades add insulation.

    Built to fit older windows

    Off-the-shelf sizes rarely fit older openings. Custom shutters and shades are measured to each window, including out-of-square frames and arched tops. Shutters can be scribed to follow uneven trim, and dormer windows often need a shallow-mount shade to clear the slope. Specialty shapes typically run 15 to 40 percent more than a standard rectangle.

    Historic district notes

    Worthington's architectural review process focuses on exterior changes, so interior shades and shutters generally don't need review. Exterior shutters or new windows are a different matter; check with the City of Worthington before changing anything on the outside of a home in the historic district. Inside, choosing white or neutral backings keeps the view from the street in keeping with the neighborhood.

    Classic looks

    Plantation shutters and Roman shades suit traditional homes, and fabric Roman shades soften rooms with lots of hard plaster surfaces. For original wavy glass you want to keep visible, inside-mounted shades that stack tightly at the top preserve the view of the window itself. Compare options in shades vs blinds vs shutters.

    Smart control without opening walls

    Rechargeable battery motors add full smart control with no new wiring, so original plaster and trim stay untouched. In mid-century homes, motorized shades are especially useful on clerestory windows that can't be reached by hand. More in our smart home window automation guide.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I add smart shades without rewiring?

    Yes. Rechargeable motors need no wiring and leave plaster and trim untouched.

    Do I need historic district approval for shades?

    Interior treatments generally don't. Exterior changes do, so check with the city for those.

    What works in a mid-century modern home?

    Motorized roller or solar shades with minimal hardware, especially for clerestory windows.

    How do you handle dormer windows?

    Shallow-mount shades or small shutters sized to clear the sloped ceiling.
