Energy-Saving and Motorized Window Treatments in Gahanna, Ohio

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

For older Gahanna homes with drafty glass, cellular shades are the best-value energy upgrade, and motorizing them on a schedule makes sure they actually close on cold nights and hot afternoons. Cordless cellular shades run about $175 to $750 per window installed, and motorized cellular about $475 to $1,400.

What makes Gahanna homes different

  • 1960s to 1980s ranches and split-levels: a large share of Gahanna's housing, often with original or early replacement windows, aluminum sliders, and large picture windows that lose heat in winter.
  • Olde Gahanna and homes near Big Walnut Creek: older houses and wooded lots where custom-fit shutters and shades suit smaller, sometimes uneven windows.
  • Newer subdivisions: better windows, but big south- and west-facing rear glass that brings in summer heat and glare.
  • Proximity to the airport and major roads: some neighborhoods hear regular traffic or flight noise. Treatments help modestly; see the FAQ below.

The insulation pick: cellular shades

Cellular, or honeycomb, shades trap air in their pockets and slow heat loss at the glass through Ohio winters. Double-cell and blackout versions insulate even more, and side channels can seal the edges for the tightest fit. Plantation shutters are a strong second, since they close tightly inside the frame.

Let automation do the work

Shades only save energy when they are closed at the right times. Motorized shades on a schedule close on cold nights and through hot summer afternoons without anyone remembering to do it, which is where the real savings come from. Rechargeable motors need no wiring. More in our smart home window automation guide.

Summer heat and glare: window film

For rooms that overheat in summer, architectural window film is worth considering alongside shades. It rejects much of the sun's heat and blocks most UV, which protects floors and furniture from fading, while keeping the view open all day. It pairs well with cellular shades for winter insulation.

Best picks by window type

  • Large picture windows: motorized cellular shades on a schedule.
  • Aluminum or older sliders: vertical cellular shades, which insulate far better than vertical blinds.
  • West-facing rear windows: solar shades or window film for heat and glare. See sunroom and hard-to-cover windows.
  • Bedrooms: blackout cellular shades, which insulate and darken.
  • Front rooms: plantation shutters for insulation and curb appeal.

Frequently asked questions

Should I replace windows or add shades first?

Shades are the cheaper fix now. If window replacement is planned within a year or two, do that first so measurements match.

Do window treatments reduce outside noise?

Modestly. Cellular shades and tight shutters soften sound but won't block it like new insulated windows.

Are double-cell shades worth it?

For drafty or north-facing windows, usually yes. They add an extra layer of trapped air for more insulation.

Does motorization really save energy?

It makes savings reliable, because shades close on schedule instead of only when someone remembers.
