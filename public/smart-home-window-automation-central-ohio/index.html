The Complete Guide to Smart Home Window Automation in Central Ohio

Updated October 2026

Smart home window automation has gone from a luxury upgrade to the way most new and remodeled Central Ohio homes now handle their windows. If you can dim your lights or set your thermostat from your phone, your shades can run the same way: rising with the sun, closing against the afternoon heat, all on their own.

This guide covers how it works, what powers it, why it matters in Ohio's climate, which rooms to start with, how to add it to a home that's already built, and what it costs here in Central Ohio.

Quick answer

Smart shades are motorized window treatments you control by voice, phone, remote, or schedule. In Central Ohio, most existing homes use rechargeable motors that need no wiring, installed in an afternoon, at roughly $450 to $1,300 per window.

How window automation works

A motorized shade has a small, quiet motor in the top rail that raises and lowers it on command. What makes it "smart" is what that motor talks to. In 2026, the big change is a standard called Matter-over-Thread. It lets one set of shades work with Apple Home, Amazon Alexa, Google Home, and SmartThings at the same time, instead of being locked to a single brand's app. That future-proofs what you install today.

Voice control and scheduling

Once your shades are on the network, you control them three ways: your voice, your phone, or a schedule. "Alexa, close the living room shades" works just like a light. But the feature people actually use most is scheduling: shades that lower at dusk for privacy, rise at sunrise, and drop through peak summer heat without anyone lifting a finger. Scenes tie it together, so one "Movie night" or "Good night" command can set shades, lights, and more at once.

Why automation pays off in Ohio's climate

Central Ohio swings from humid summers to cold, gray winters, and windows are where a lot of that comes through. In summer, south- and west-facing glass can turn a room into a greenhouse by late afternoon; a schedule that lowers solar or light-filtering shades before the heat hits keeps rooms cooler and protects floors and furniture from fading. In winter, insulating cellular shades that close automatically at sunset trap a layer of air against cold glass, cutting drafts and heat loss overnight. The benefit isn't the motor itself, it's that the shades actually get used at the right time every day.

Which rooms to automate first

You don't have to motorize every window. Most Central Ohio homeowners start with:

  • Great rooms and two-story windows that are hard or impossible to reach by hand.
  • West-facing living spaces that overheat in the afternoon.
  • Bedrooms, for blackout shades on a wake-up schedule.
  • Media rooms and home offices, for glare control on screens.
  • Sunrooms, which get the most heat and glare in the house. See our sunroom window treatment guide.

Cordless manual shades elsewhere keep the budget in check and keep the look consistent.

Powering your shades

There are four options. Rechargeable battery motors install with no wiring, which makes them ideal for a finished home, and usually go several months between charges. Plug-in works where there's an outlet nearby. Hardwired is the clean, permanent choice during a remodel or new build. Solar-powered motors charge off the window's own light for sunny spots that are hard to reach.

Retrofitting a home that's already built

This is the one most Central Ohio homeowners care about: you don't need to open walls. Rechargeable battery-powered motorized shades add full automation to an existing home with no wiring at all. They're measured to each window, installed in an afternoon, and running on your phone the same day.

Planning for new construction

Building in a growing area like New Albany, Powell, or Dublin? Bring up shades before drywall. Running low-voltage wire to each window during framing is inexpensive then and costly later, and it lets you choose hardwired motors with no batteries to ever charge.

Safety for kids and pets

Motorized shades have no cords at all, which removes the strangulation hazard that comes with corded blinds. Learn more in our guide to child- and pet-safe window treatments.

What it costs in Central Ohio

Installed, motorized rechargeable roller shades typically run $450 to $1,300 per window, and hardwired $650 to $1,600, depending on size and fabric. Motorized cellular shades run about $475 to $1,400. A typical 12-window home runs around $6,500 to $12,000. Frequently asked questions

Do smart shades work with Alexa, Google Home, and Apple Home?

Yes. Most systems connect through a small bridge, and Matter-compatible systems can work with all the major platforms at once.

What happens if the Wi-Fi goes down?

Your shades still work from their remote or wall switch. Schedules and voice control pick back up when the network returns.

Are motorized shades loud?

Modern shade motors are quiet, closer to a soft hum than a garage door.

Can I motorize shades I already have?

Usually not. Motors are built into the shade's roller or headrail, so automation typically means new custom shades made for each window.
