Motorized Shades and Custom Window Treatments in Powell, Ohio

Updated October 2026

Quick answer

Powell homes tend to have open floor plans, two-story family rooms, and sunny morning rooms. Motorize anything that needs a ladder, use solar shades where glare and heat are the problem, and keep one fabric across each open living area so it reads as one space. Motorized shades run about $450 to $1,300 per window installed.

What makes Powell homes different

  • 1990s to 2010s subdivisions: much of Powell and surrounding Liberty Township, including areas like Golf Village and Scioto Reserve, was built in this period. Expect open kitchens flowing into family rooms, two-story entries, and big rear windows.
  • Morning rooms, sunrooms, and bonus rooms: very common in Powell floor plans and often the brightest, hottest rooms in the house.
  • Golf course and wooded lot views: many homes back onto fairways, ponds, or trees. The goal is controlling glare without losing the view.
  • Downtown Powell: a smaller set of older homes near the historic center, where custom-fit shutters and shades suit older windows.

High windows and great rooms

If a window needs a ladder, motorize it. Motorized roller or cellular shades run by remote, app, voice, or a sunrise and sunset schedule. Solar-powered motors suit sunny high windows where wiring isn't practical, and rechargeable motors work almost anywhere else. More in our smart home window automation guide.

Getting open floor plans right

In an open layout, every window is seen together, so mismatched treatments stand out more than in a closed floor plan. A few rules make it look intentional:

  • Use one fabric and one hardware finish across the whole open area.
  • Align bottom rails at the same height, which motorized scenes handle automatically.
  • Group every motorized shade in the space into one scene so it opens and closes as a single wall.
  • If the kitchen needs something different, such as faux wood blinds near the sink, keep the color matched to the shades nearby.

Sunrooms and morning rooms

Solar shades cut glare and heat while keeping the view, and a schedule can lower them automatically during the hottest afternoon hours. For rooms with glass on three sides, motorizing them as a group saves walking from window to window. See sunroom and hard-to-cover windows.

Best picks by room

  • Two-story family rooms and entries: motorized roller or cellular shades.
  • Morning rooms and sunrooms: solar shades, motorized as a group.
  • Kids' rooms: cordless blackout cellular shades. See child and pet safe options.
  • Front rooms and home offices: plantation shutters or light-filtering shades.

Frequently asked questions

How do you cover high windows?

Motorized shades, run by remote, app, voice, or schedule. No ladder and usually no new wiring.

What works best in a morning room?

Solar shades, which cut heat and glare but keep the view.

How do I keep an open floor plan looking consistent?

One fabric, aligned bottom rails, and all motorized shades grouped into one scene.

Can I motorize just the high windows?

Yes. Many homeowners motorize only the windows they can't reach and use cordless shades everywhere else.
