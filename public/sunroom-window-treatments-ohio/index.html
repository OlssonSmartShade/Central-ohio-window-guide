Choosing Window Treatments for Ohio Sunrooms and Hard-to-Cover Windows

Updated October 2026

Sunrooms, two-story great rooms, arched windows, and skylights are where off-the-shelf treatments fail. Central Ohio summers turn a glass room into an oven, winters make it cold, and high windows are impossible to reach. Here's how to handle each one.

Sunrooms

Solar shades are the go-to. Their screen fabric cuts glare, heat, and UV fading while keeping your view of the yard. Choose a lower openness for more heat control or a higher openness for more view. Cellular shades add insulation if the room is used in winter. With many windows in one room, motorizing them so they all move together at the touch of a button or on a schedule makes a big difference.

High and two-story windows

If you need a ladder to reach it, motorize it. Motorized roller or cellular shades can be controlled by remote, app, voice, or a sunrise/sunset schedule. Solar-powered motors work well on sunny high windows where wiring isn't practical. See our smart home window automation guide for power options.

Arched, angled, and specialty shapes

Custom plantation shutters can be built to fit arches, circles, and angled tops exactly. Specialty shapes typically cost 15 to 40 percent more than a standard rectangle because each one is built to the exact opening.

Oversized windows and sliding doors

Wide roller shades, vertical cellular shades, and sliding panel tracks handle big openings cleanly. Motorization helps here too, since large shades are heavy to raise by hand.

Window film as an alternative

When you don't want to cover the glass at all, architectural window film reduces heat, glare, and UV while keeping the view completely open. It pairs well with shades for rooms that get intense afternoon sun.
